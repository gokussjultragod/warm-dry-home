Heated airers run at low temperatures and low power, so many people leave them on overnight. Whether that is a good idea depends on your model and how you use it.

Short answer: check your model's instructions first: some manufacturers say not to leave it unattended. Fire safety advice is generally to switch off electrical heating appliances when you go to bed or leave the house. If you do dry overnight, use a model with a timer, plug it directly into a wall socket, keep it away from curtains and bedding, and never cover it with anything except its own cover.

What makes heated airers fairly low risk

  • The rails only get warm, typically well under the temperature of a radiator.
  • Power is low, usually 200 to 300 W, so the wiring and plug carry much less current than a heater.
  • Good models have thermal cut-outs that switch off if they overheat.

Risks to avoid

  • Extension leads and adaptors: plug straight into the wall.
  • Damaged cables or plugs: stop using it if the cable is cracked or the plug gets hot.
  • Using a non-matching cover: only use the cover designed for your airer; blankets or duvets can trap heat.
  • Placing it near curtains, beds or sofas.
  • Cheap unbranded models without UKCA or CE marking from unknown sellers.

A safer way to dry overnight

Use an airer with a built-in timer and set it to switch off after a few hours. Put it in a small room with the door closed and a dehumidifier running on its humidistat. The clothes keep drying after the airer switches off, and the moisture doesn't end up on your walls. See drying clothes with a dehumidifier.

Where to buy (prices change often, so check the current price):

  • Heated airers with a built-in timer
  • Timer plugs
  • Smoke alarms

And make sure you have a working smoke alarm on every floor. Many UK fire services offer free home fire safety visits.

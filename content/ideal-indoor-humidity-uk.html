Most damp and mould problems come down to one number: relative humidity. Once you know yours, it is much easier to decide whether you need to change habits, buy a dehumidifier or call a landlord.

Short answer: aim for 40 to 60% relative humidity indoors, ideally around 50% in winter. Above about 65% for long periods, mould and dust mites start to thrive. Below 30% the air can feel dry on skin and eyes.

How to measure it

A digital hygrometer (a small thermometer that also shows humidity) costs only a few pounds and is the most useful thing you can buy before spending money on anything else. Put one in your bedroom and one in the living room, away from windows, radiators and the kitchen. Check them in the morning and the evening for a week.

What your readings mean

ReadingWhat it meansWhat to do
Below 35%Dry airRare in UK homes in winter; turn the dehumidifier down or off
40 to 60%HealthyNothing, keep your habits
60 to 70%Too dampVentilate more, dry clothes differently, consider a dehumidifier
Above 70%Mould riskDehumidifier plus finding the source of the moisture

Short peaks after a shower or cooking are normal. What matters is where the reading settles a couple of hours later.

Bringing humidity down

  • Use extractor fans and keep the bathroom door closed while showering.
  • Open trickle vents and air rooms for 10 to 15 minutes each morning.
  • Dry washing in one closed room with a window cracked, or with a dehumidifier.
  • Keep the home at a steady temperature; cold rooms have higher relative humidity.
  • Run a dehumidifier with a humidistat set to 50 to 55% so it only works when needed. See which type to choose.

A dehumidifier often runs for hours every day in winter, so it is fair to ask what it adds to your electricity bill. The answer is usually less than people fear, as long as you pick the right type and use the humidistat.

Short answer: most home compressor dehumidifiers use about 150 to 250 W, which is about 4p to 6p an hour. Desiccant models use about 300 to 650 W, roughly 8p to 16p an hour. With a humidistat set to 50 to 55%, a typical room costs a few pounds to a little over £10 a month.

Compared with other appliances

  • A compressor dehumidifier uses roughly the same power as a large TV or a fridge-freezer working hard.
  • One hour of a 2 kW electric heater costs about the same as eight to ten hours of a compressor dehumidifier.
  • One load in a vented tumble dryer costs about the same as 20 hours of a 200 W dehumidifier.

Five ways to pay less

  1. Use the humidistat. Set 50 to 55% and let it switch itself off.
  2. Pick the right type. Compressor for heated rooms, desiccant for cold ones. See which type suits you.
  3. Close the door of the room it is drying.
  4. Clean the filter every couple of weeks so air flows freely.
  5. Run it off-peak if you are on a time-of-use tariff.

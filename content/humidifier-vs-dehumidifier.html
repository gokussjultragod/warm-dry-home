The names are almost the same, but they do opposite jobs. Buy the wrong one and you either waste electricity or make a damp problem worse. Here is how to tell which one your home needs.

Short answer: a dehumidifier takes water out of the air and is what most UK homes need in winter, when windows stream with condensation and mould appears. A humidifier adds water to the air and only helps if your home is genuinely too dry, below about 35% humidity. Most humidifiers use very little electricity, around 1p an hour.

How to tell which one you need

Don't guess. A digital hygrometer costs a few pounds and shows the humidity in a room. Leave one in the bedroom and one in the living room for a week and check the readings in the morning and evening.

  • Above 60% most of the time: you need less moisture. Ventilate more and consider a dehumidifier. See where to put one and what it costs to run.
  • 40 to 60%: healthy. You don't need either.
  • Below about 35% for long periods: a humidifier may help. This is uncommon in UK homes, but can happen in well-insulated flats with electric heating, or in a single room where the heating runs a lot.

Why most UK homes need a dehumidifier, not a humidifier

British winter air outside is often 80 to 90% humidity. Indoors, cooking, showers, breathing and drying clothes add several litres of water a day. With windows shut against the cold, that moisture has nowhere to go and ends up on the coldest surfaces: windows, outside walls and the backs of wardrobes. That is why condensation and black mould are so common, and why adding more moisture with a humidifier usually makes things worse.

Types of humidifier, if you do need one

  • Ultrasonic: makes a fine cool mist with very little power. Use distilled or filtered water in hard-water areas to avoid white dust on furniture.
  • Evaporative: a fan blows air through a wet filter. Low power and it can't over-humidify easily, but filters need replacing.
  • Warm mist (steam): boils the water, so it uses more electricity and the steam can scald. Not a good choice around children or pets.

Whichever type, clean the tank regularly as the instructions say. Stagnant water can grow bacteria that the humidifier then sprays into the room.

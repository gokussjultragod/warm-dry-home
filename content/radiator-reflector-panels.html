Radiator reflector panels are thin, shiny sheets that sit on the wall behind a radiator. They are one of the cheapest heating upgrades you can buy, but they help some homes a lot more than others. Here is how to tell if yours is one of them.

Short answer: reflectors are worth fitting behind radiators on outside walls without insulation, typically solid brick walls in pre-1930s homes or uninsulated cavity walls. They bounce heat back into the room instead of letting it warm the bricks and escape outside. On internal walls or well-insulated walls the difference is small, so don't bother there.

How they work

A radiator warms the room mostly by heating the air around it, but it also radiates heat backwards into the wall behind it. On a cold, uninsulated outside wall that heat soaks into the brickwork and is lost outside. A reflector panel has an insulating layer to slow that loss and a reflective face to send radiant heat back into the room.

Which radiators to do first

Radiator on…Worth it?
Outside wall, solid brick (older homes)Yes, the biggest benefit
Outside wall, uninsulated cavityYes
Outside wall with cavity or external insulationSmall benefit
Internal wall between two heated roomsNo, the heat stays in the house anyway

Not sure what your walls are? Brick walls laid with some bricks showing their short ends usually mean solid walls. If your home was built after about the 1930s, it probably has cavity walls, and your energy performance certificate (EPC) says whether they are insulated.

How to fit one in ten minutes

  1. Turn the radiator off and let it cool completely.
  2. Measure the radiator's width and height, and cut the panel slightly smaller with scissors. Leave room for the brackets.
  3. Put double-sided tape or the supplied pads on the back.
  4. Slide it down behind the radiator, shiny side facing the room, and press it onto the wall with a ruler or a long stick.
  5. Turn the radiator back on.

Other cheap ways to keep the heat in

  • Keep sofas and long curtains from covering radiators.
  • Bleed radiators that are cold at the top.

Opening the wardrobe to a musty smell, white or green spots on a coat, or black marks on the back panel is depressingly common in British homes in winter. The good news: the causes are simple, and so are most of the fixes.

Short answer: wardrobes on cold outside walls trap still, damp air. Pull the wardrobe a few centimetres away from the wall, only put away completely dry clothes, leave the doors open for a while each day, and bring the room's humidity down to around 50%. Wash affected clothes at the highest temperature the label allows and clean the wardrobe with a mould remover.

Why wardrobes get mouldy

  • Cold outside walls: the back of the wardrobe and the wall behind it are the coldest surfaces in the room, so moisture condenses there.
  • No air movement: closed doors and packed rails mean damp air never leaves.
  • Clothes put away slightly damp after drying indoors.
  • A humid bedroom: people breathing overnight with the window and vents closed. See what humidity your home should be.

Cleaning clothes and shoes

  • Take everything out, outdoors if you can, and brush off loose mould outside so spores don't spread indoors. Wear a mask.
  • Wash fabrics at the hottest temperature the label allows (60°C kills most mould). Adding a laundry cleanser made for mould can help.
  • Dry clothes completely before putting them back.
  • Wipe leather shoes and bags with a cloth dampened with a suitable leather or mould cleaner, then let them dry naturally.
  • Throw away anything that still smells musty after washing, or that has deep staining you can't remove.

Cleaning the wardrobe

Empty it, wipe all inside surfaces and the back panel with a mould remover suitable for the material, then leave the doors open until it is fully dry. Clean the wall behind it too. Our guide to removing mould safely covers products and safety.

Stop it coming back

  1. Move the wardrobe 5 to 10 cm away from outside walls, or onto an internal wall if possible.
  2. Don't overpack the rail. Air needs to move between clothes.
  3. Leave the wardrobe doors open for an hour or two each day, especially in the morning.
  4. Only put away clothes that are bone dry.
  5. Use a moisture absorber or reusable dehumidifier bag inside the wardrobe.
  6. Keep the bedroom at a steady temperature and around 50% humidity. A small desiccant dehumidifier works well in cold bedrooms.

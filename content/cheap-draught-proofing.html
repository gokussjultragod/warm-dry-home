Before you spend money on a heater, stop the warm air you have already paid for from leaking out. Draught-proofing is one of the cheapest ways to make a British home feel warmer, and most of it takes an afternoon with no tools beyond scissors and a screwdriver.

Short answer: start with the gaps you can feel: around doors, letterboxes, keyholes, loose windows, floorboards and unused chimneys. Self-adhesive foam or brush strips, a letterbox brush, a door draught excluder and a chimney balloon cost little and can make a room noticeably warmer. Never block trickle vents, air bricks or the ventilation in rooms with a gas fire, boiler or open flue.

How to find draughts

On a windy day, move the back of your hand slowly around door frames, window edges, skirting boards and the loft hatch. You will feel the cold air. A lit incense stick works too: the smoke bends where air is moving. Write the spots down and fix the biggest ones first.

What not to block

  • Trickle vents at the top of windows: they stop condensation and mould. See stopping condensation.
  • Air bricks in outside walls, which keep floors and walls dry.
  • Extractor fans in kitchens and bathrooms.
  • Any vent in a room with a gas fire, boiler or open-flued appliance. These need fresh air to burn safely. If in doubt, ask a Gas Safe registered engineer, and make sure you have a carbon monoxide alarm.

Draught-proofing is about stopping uncontrolled gaps. Controlled ventilation is what keeps your home healthy, especially if you dry washing indoors.

Renting?

Most of these fixes are removable and don't damage the property: stick-on strips, freestanding excluders, curtains and chimney balloons. Ask your landlord before screwing anything to doors or frames, and report broken seals or rotten frames, which are their responsibility.

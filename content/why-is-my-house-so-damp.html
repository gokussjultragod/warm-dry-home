A damp home feels cold, smells musty and grows mould. Before buying anything, work out where the water is coming from. Most of the time it is everyday condensation, which is cheap to fix. Sometimes it is a leak or a building problem that needs a repair.

Short answer: in most UK homes, winter damp is condensation: moisture from cooking, showers, drying clothes and breathing that settles on cold walls and windows. Fix it with ventilation, steady heating and, if needed, a dehumidifier. Damp that appears in one patch, stays in summer or rises from the floor usually points to a leak, rising or penetrating damp, which needs a repair.

A quick way to check

  1. Buy a cheap digital hygrometer and check readings in each room for a week. Readings that sit above 60 to 65% point to condensation. Our guide to ideal indoor humidity explains the numbers.
  2. Tape a small square of kitchen foil to the damp wall, sealed on all sides, and leave it 24 to 48 hours. Moisture on the room side of the foil means condensation; moisture underneath means the damp is coming from inside the wall.
  3. Note whether it gets worse after rain (penetrating damp or leaks) or after showers and drying clothes (condensation).

When to call in help

If the damp is in one spot, gets worse after rain or is low on ground-floor walls, get it checked before treating the symptoms. Be cautious of anyone who recommends expensive chemical damp-proofing before ruling out condensation, leaks and blocked air bricks. If you rent, report it to your landlord in writing.
